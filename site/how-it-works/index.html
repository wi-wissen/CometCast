Where your data goes

The picture goes straight from your device to the screen. A small server only helps the two find each other.

What the relay sees and stores

The relay is a small server that matches a device and a screen by their room code and passes along the messages they need to set up the connection. It sees the room code, the name of the screen and your network address (to slow down abuse). It keeps rooms in memory only and stores nothing on disk. Pictures, files and links never go through the public relay.

Guest Wi-Fi and the internal network

The classic case: you are in the guest Wi-Fi, the screen is in the organisation's internal network. Finding each other always works, because both reach the server over the internet. The picture needs a way between the two networks, and those networks are usually kept apart on purpose.

  • Same Wi-Fi. Your device and the screen talk directly. Built-in ways such as AirPlay and Cast work too. Some networks keep devices apart even in one Wi-Fi ("client isolation"); then it is the next case.
  • Different networks, no own server. Comet Cast learns the public address of each side and tries to go out to the internet and back in. That works only if the router of your organisation sends such traffic back inside (called "hairpinning"). Many company and school firewalls do not, often on purpose. Then no connection comes up, and the app says so. Built-in ways never work across networks.
  • Different networks, your own server. Your organisation runs a Comet Cast server with a TURN relay that both networks can reach, for example in the DMZ. The picture travels from your device to that server and on to the screen. This always works and the picture stays inside. The screen shows the address and QR code of your server, so people who scan it land there; nobody sets anything up on their own device.

From mobile data or another site it is the same idea: Comet Cast connects over the internet, which usually works; strict networks need your own server. The public relay never carries the picture; it has no TURN relay on purpose. When you need your own server.

Screens nearby and signed announcements

Screens in your network announce themselves so that the app can list them. Every announcement is signed with a key that belongs to the screen, so another device on the network cannot pretend to be it, remove it from your list or change what it says. When a device joins with a name that another device used before, the screen shows a neutral note.

The connection check code

After two devices connect, both show the same short code. If the codes match, nobody sat in between. You do not have to compare them, but you can.
